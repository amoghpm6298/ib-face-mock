**How it would run**

Ten percent get only the statement SMS.

Everyone else is split into **never started a request** and **already started a request**.

We will not message between 9 at night and 9 in the morning. If a send would fall in that window, it waits until 9 in the morning.

---

**People who never started**

We need them to start and finish EMI on this outstanding, before the due date.

**If they do not have the app**  
The first job is to get the app on, then take them to EMI on this bill — not a generic “download our app.”  
We send as soon as we are allowed to, once the statement is out — not three days later.  
They already got SMS, so the first campaign message is **WhatsApp**.  
If WhatsApp does not land, we try **in-app** if they might still see the app, otherwise one email. We do not send a second SMS.  
If they do not open WhatsApp, we try once more at a **different time of day** (morning vs evening), still in the first day or two, on in-app or email. If they still never show up, we leave them until a last message before the due date.  
If they open but do not install, we show how to install, still on WhatsApp.  
If they install, we treat them **at once** like someone who already had the app. We do not wait for the next batch.  
If they convert at any point, they leave the campaign.

**If they have the app**  
Same day, in allowed hours: **in-app and WhatsApp** together. Email is not the first message.  
The first message is not a test. It simply says: this statement can go on EMI, in the app, now. In festive, we contrast that with EMI at the shop — this is the bill they already have.

Then we watch what they do.

If they convert, they are out. No more messages.

If the message did not reach them, we try the other channel (WhatsApp or in-app) at the next allowed time, with the same ask. If both fail, we stop trying to reach them. We do not test copy on people who never saw us.

If it reached them but they never opened it, we try the other channel at the other time of day, still early. If they still do not open, we wait for the last message before due date. We do not keep sending every two days.

If they **opened and did not click**, they saw us and did not start. **This is the only place I would run a test**, because you could not rank the two reasons, and we have no old campaign to tell us:  
- one group: where to do it in the app  
- the other group: cost — why this EMI versus paying in full, and versus shop EMI  

We send that test in the **same or next allowed slot**. They were just on the phone. We stay on the **channel they opened**. We do not wait two days.

If they do not open the test, we treat it as a reach problem once — change channel and time — then we stop repeating.  
If they open again and still do not click, they are saying no. One last message about the due date, on the channel they actually read, then we stop. We do not send “how to” again and again.  
If they **click**, the test is over. They are now someone who started and did not finish. Both test groups are treated the same from here.

If they **clicked but did not convert**, they meant to do it. We do not sell them “why EMI.” We ask them to finish, **in the app**, the same day if we are allowed to message.  
If they click again and still do not convert, one more finish reminder, then we stop. We cannot see where they dropped in the screens, so we should not guess.  
If they never come back after the first click, one reminder on the other channel, next evening in allowed hours, then we stop.

---

**People who already started a request**

The job is only to **finish**. We do not run the cost-versus-awareness test on them. That would be the wrong campaign.

Same day, in allowed hours: **in-app** to go back to the open request, and WhatsApp once if they do not open in-app. We do not upsell in festive. We ask them to complete before the due date.

If they convert, they are out.

If the message does not reach them or they do not open, we follow the same reach rules as above — try the other channel, other time, do not nag on a channel they ignored.

If they open or click and the request is still not successful: a short “come back and finish” in the app. If they stall again, one WhatsApp, then we stop.

If they drop the request completely, they can be treated like someone who never started. We do not lecture them on cost while a request is already open.

---

**Near the due date**

About three days before due, **only people who never engaged at all** get one last message, on the best channel we have not used yet, in the evening before 9 at night. Festive does not extend the due date.  
People who already said no, or clicked twice and still did not convert, do **not** get a last-chance blast.  
After the due date, we stop. The next statement is a different campaign.

---

**How we will know if this worked**  
We compare against the 10% who only got the statement SMS.  
We look separately at never-started vs already-started — that is your split, and we have never proven it in a campaign.  
We look at whether they converted after the first message, after the test, after the finish reminder, or after the last due-date message.  
We look at which channel and which time of day.  
We look at success by outstanding size, so the “bigger bills” feel can become a number. We still will not use a rupee cutoff to decide who enters.
